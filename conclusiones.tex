In this study, we performed an experimental characterization of the effects of BS on the UWB range estimation in the 6.5 GHz band. The measurements provide body-placement and scenario specific evidence for this frequency. The main conclusions are:

The physical placement of the tag also contributes significantly to the ranging reliability under NLOS. The head placement has the best indoor NLOS results, MAE = 18.66 cm, and also the best outdoor LOS result (MAE = 4.87 cm). The wrist is the best outdoor NLOS placement with MAE = 24.07 cm. The hip placed in the neighboring area is most impacted because it is 97.76 cm indoor and 94.78 cm outdoors under NLOS, while the chest is 89.49 cm indoors and 79.00 cm outdoors. For safety-critical tracking, belt- or torso-mounted tags must therefore have bias compensation or complementary localization information.

The 6.5 GHz measurements revealed that there was a constructive multipath pattern in the indoor corridor. For many placements, such as head, hand, wrist, knee and ankle, indoor NLOS errors were lower than outdoor NLOS errors. This shows that the reflections on the corridor can help to keep ToF observability when the body blocks the direct path. Indoor network planning can take this as part of the design of a network, but the effect of indoor walls is placement- and geometry-dependent.

The zero-mean Gaussian assumption was not suitable for the absolute ranging errors observed on BS samples. Distribution fit of more than 100,000 independent range samples indicated the transition from gamma-like LOS errors to heavy-tailed log-normal NLOS errors. Localization/tracking algorithms which estimate those measurements with a zero-mean Gaussian observation model might thus cause a biased estimate without NLOS detection or bias-aware compensation.

These findings provide a reference for future industrial tracking based on body-worn UWB devices. Future research should include bias and distribution-aware ranging observation models. The next step is to design algorithms to detect UWB link quality in real time and update the observation model in LOS and NLOS settings.
