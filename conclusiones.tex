This study presented an experimental characterization of BS effects on UWB range estimation in the 6.5 GHz band. The measurements provide body-placement and scenario-specific evidence for this frequency. The main conclusions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tag Placement Hierarchy:} The physical placement of the tag strongly affects ranging reliability under NLOS conditions. The head placement provided the best indoor NLOS result, with MAE = 18.66 cm, and one of the best outdoor LOS results, with MAE = 4.87 cm. The wrist was the best outdoor NLOS placement, with MAE = 24.07 cm. Torso-adjacent placements were the most affected: the hip reached MAE = 97.76 cm indoors and 94.78 cm outdoors under NLOS, while the chest reached 89.49 cm indoors and 79.00 cm outdoors. For safety-critical tracking, belt- or torso-mounted tags therefore require bias compensation or complementary localization information.

    \item \textbf{Frequency-Specific Propagation and Constructive Multipath:} The 6.5 GHz measurements showed a constructive multipath pattern in the indoor corridor. For several placements, including the head, hand, wrist, knee, and ankle, indoor NLOS errors were lower than outdoor NLOS errors. This suggests that corridor reflections can partially preserve ToF observability when the body blocks the direct path. Indoor network planning can use this behavior as a design consideration, although the effect remains placement- and geometry-dependent.

    \item \textbf{Statistical Error Modeling and Gaussian Ranging Assumptions:} The zero-mean Gaussian assumption was not adequate for the absolute ranging errors observed under BS. Distribution fitting over more than 100,000 independent range samples showed a change from gamma-like LOS errors to heavy-tailed log-normal NLOS errors. Localization or tracking algorithms that process these measurements with a zero-mean Gaussian observation model may therefore produce biased estimates unless NLOS detection or bias-aware compensation is incorporated.
\end{enumerate}

These findings offer an empirical reference for future industrial tracking solutions based on body-worn UWB devices. Future research should incorporate bias- and distribution-aware ranging observation models. A practical next step is to develop algorithms that diagnose UWB link quality in real time and adapt the observation model between LOS and NLOS conditions.
