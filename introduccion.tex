Indoor Positioning Systems (IPS) have been used in a number of human-oriented applications including safety in the workplace, navigation and asset tracking. Ultra-Wideband (UWB) is often selected because it can provide accurate ToF ranging under controlled propagation conditions \cite{ref6, ref8}, and once the mobile node is worn on the body, performance can drastically change. One such degradation is body shadowing (BS) that occurs when the user's body obstructs or alters the propagation path between the tag and the anchor \cite{ref16}.

In this paper, the tag is the body-worn mobile node and the anchor is the fixed reference node. Figure~\ref{fig:system_model} illustrates the BS condition by comparing the LOS and NLOS links between the two nodes. Biological tissue has a high permittivity due to its water content, so it can attenuate, reflect, refract and diffract radio frequencies, and due to this range of radio frequencies they can introduce ranging errors that reduce the reliability of the system for applications like industrial safety \cite{Kianfar2020}. 

Previous studies have measured this effect in high-accuracy systems. In LOS (Line-of-Sight) cases, the reported errors are usually lower and in the decimetric range depending on the placement of the tag and the experimental setup \cite{ref4, ref16, ref18}. In NLOS (Non-Line-of-Sight) cases the losses were larger: mean absolute errors between 1.2 m and over 2 m were reported for those body placements that were affected by BS \cite{ref4}. While some mitigation strategies have been proposed, most studies have focused on the 3-5 GHz range \cite{ref4}. The 6.5 GHz band is less frequently used for body-worn UWB ranging and is still applicable to the experiment. In ToF-based ranging, the temporal resolution is mainly determined by pulse duration and receiver timing and not wavelength alone, and picosecond-scale pulses can be used for finer estimates of ∆t, but it is a practical situation. The operation at this band can also increase attenuation in biological tissues and a resolution of the BS effect would be crucial to measure directly. For this reason, we concentrate on the ranging error in BS and on the probability distributions describing the measured absolute error. In the collected data the LOS errors stayed mostly in the centimeter-to-decimeter range, while BS-induced NLOS conditions increased the MAE to 97.76 cm and produced maximum errors of 2.9 m for unsuitable torso placements.

The purpose of this paper is to examine the effects of BS on the 1D range estimation of UWB systems running at 6.5 GHz. This work extends initial work \cite{danny_daniel_and_victor_quintero} with an expanded body placement analysis and statistical treatment in scenarios. The main contributions are:

Body Placement Analysis: Seven on-body tags are compared under the same measurement protocol (head, chest, hip, wrist, hand, ankle, and knee). The MAE, standard deviation and maximum-error values are used to make a placement hierarchy for body-worn tracking applications.

Statistical Ranging Error Modeling: Goodness of fit and non-parametric tests are used to compare positive-support models for absolute ranging error. We note that the gamma-like LOS errors are shifted to the heavy-tailed log-normal NLOS errors which is relevant for filters with Gaussian observation assumptions.


6.5 GHz Indoor/Outdoor Characterization: We compare the indoor corridor and open field at 6.5 GHz. The results show that the reflected indoor part will partially preserve the range of the limb-mounted tags under BS.
