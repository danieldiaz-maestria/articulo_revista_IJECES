    \textbf{Abstract - }	\textit{Ultra-Wideband (UWB) is widely used in IPS due to its short pulses that allow for accurate range estimation. However, it is found that the human body may obstruct the radio link and expose body shadowing (BS) error in body-worn tracking. Here, we present an experimental characterization of BS in a 1D UWB ranging link at 6.5 GHz, which is a band in which body-placement measurements are less frequently reported for 3-5 GHz systems. A fixed anchor and a body-worn tag were used to evaluate 7 placements: head, chest, hip, wrist, hand, ankle, knee. More than 100,000 independent Time-of-Flight (ToF) range samples were collected under Line-of-Sight (LOS) and Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) conditions in an indoor corridor and outdoor open field. We can observe a clear dependence of tag placement. Head placement resulted in MAE of 4.87 cm in outdoor LOS and 18.66 cm in indoor NLOS, respectively, while indoor LOS measurements varied across placements, with MAE values of 10.43 cm at the head, 16.49 cm at the chest and 7.19 cm at the hip. The placements of the torso were the most impacted by NLOS; the maximum reported MAE was 97.76 cm. One can see that NLOS absolute errors are better captured by a heavy-tailed log-normal model, not the Gaussian approximation often used in probabilistic ranging models. The indoor corridor also yielded lower NLOS errors in our experiment than the outdoor setup for a number of limb-mounted tags, suggesting that reflected components may partially preserve ToF observability when the direct path is blocked. These measurements provide experimental support for body-placement-aware and distribution-aware UWB ranging algorithms.}
