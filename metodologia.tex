The research was conducted according to a Data-Driven Methodology that was utilized to systematically characterize the statistical models of the ranging error caused by BS.

Hardware Configuration. The experiments were performed with Qorvo DWM1001 development modules, with a center frequency of 6.5 GHz (Channel 5) \cite{ref8}. We have one anchor and one tag, which were the human participant.

System Model Description. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:system_model}, the anchor is placed at a known position, and the tag is placed on the different parts of the participant's body. The ranging process was based on ToF measurements between the tag and the anchor in LOS and NLOS. This configuration captures the direct impact of BS on ranging error while keeping a controlled and reproducible setup.

Experimental Scenarios. Measurements were collected in two sites with different propagation conditions:

Scenario 1 (Outdoor Open Field): Static measurements were taken in an open field of approximately 20~m × 30~m. We used this site as the reduced-reflection case where the ranging error is mainly related to BS and ToF estimation with weak environmental multipath.

Scenario 2 (Indoor Corridor): The indoor measurements were carried out in a corridor about 2~m wide and 50~m long, with walls, floor, and ceiling close by. This site provided a reflection-rich case where reflected, diffracted, and refracted components can affect attenuation, first-path detection, ToF estimation, and the resulting ranging error.

The ranging characterization protocol evaluated seven body positions for the tag to represent a typical wearable use-case (head, chest, hip, hand, wrist, knee, and ankle) from Fig. \ref{fig:system_model} and Fig. \ref{fig:posiciones} to estimate the range of the tag to a single anchor with time of flight (ToF) signal between the tag and the anchor. The measurements were collected in two propagation settings: LOS (Line-of-Sight) where the tag and the anchor are directly connected and NLOS (Non-Line-of-Sight) where BS is induced when the participant's body is rotated 180 degrees in the direction the tag and anchor are located.

Although the attenuation was not used as the main ranging metric, it was considered a relevant propagation factor since severe NLOS attenuation, especially in outdoor situations, can restrict the link reliability between tag and anchor and affect the ToF estimation quality.

Measurements were performed at 13 static positions in 1~m increments, and the distances from 1~m to 13~m from the anchor were taken from positions. At each position, 250 independent distance measurements were taken for each channel condition, LOS and NLOS, for each body placement and for each scenario, resulting in over 100,000 measurements (13 positions × 7 body placements × 2 channel conditions × 250 samples × 2 scenarios). From this huge amount of data, we can construct very good statistical models to characterize the distribution of ranging errors under each condition.

The absolute error for each measurement was ${e_i = |d_{\text{measured},i} - d_{\text{true},i}|}$. Because of the positive nature of the measurements, the distributions had a positive support, such as the gamma and log-normal distributions. Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) were used for quantitatively selecting the most accurate underlying distribution model. The Gaussian assumption was only used as a reference hypothesis which is often used in Gaussian noise models (Shapiro-Wilk test and Q-Q plots) but not as the final model for absolute error. Other candidates like Weibull or Rayleigh can also model positive errors and will be useful for more detailed analysis. Student's t is more appropriate for symmetric signed residuals and was not considered in this absolute-error analysis. In addition, non-parametric inferential tests (Mann-Whitney U test) were performed to evaluate whether differences in placements and conditions were statistically significant.
